## Why a CEO should read this

You don't have a technology problem. You have a **decision-making** problem with technology at the centre of it — which platform to bet on, what to build versus buy, where the next dollar of margin hides, and whether the team you have can take you where the board wants to go.

Most companies answer that by hiring an expensive full-time CTO, or by leaving the call to whoever shouts loudest in the engineering room. There is a third option that more boards are choosing: bring in someone who has already owned the whole problem — the building, the billing, the infrastructure, the financing and the exit — and rent the judgement, not the headcount.

> "I've signed the front of the cheques and the back of them. I've built the product, run the network it rode on, and financed the customers who bought it. There aren't many technology decisions I'm seeing for the first time."

## What a fractional CTO engagement actually delivers

- **Strategy — Technology roadmap tied to the P&L.** A clear 12–24 month plan where every technology bet maps to revenue, cost or risk — written in language the board understands, not jargon.
- **Architecture — Build vs. buy, decided correctly.** Hard-won judgement on what to build, what to buy, and what to kill — so you stop paying for the wrong things and over-engineering the rest.
- **Team — Hiring, leading & levelling up engineers.** Recruit the right people, fix the structure, mentor the leads, and know when you genuinely need a full-timer versus a contractor.
- **Vendors — Vendor & cost control.** From an operator who has *been* the vendor — I negotiate contracts, audit cloud and SaaS spend, and cut the fat without breaking the system.
- **Risk — Security, uptime & compliance.** Infrastructure that stays up, data that stays safe, and the governance the board needs to sleep — grounded in years of running networks and regulated finance.
- **Diligence — Investment & M&A readiness.** Technical due diligence, scalability assessments and the credibility that reassures investors and acquirers your technology is an asset, not a liability.

## Why fractional works — the board-level case

A full-time CTO at the seniority you actually want costs a base salary, equity, bonus, super and the long tail of recruitment and ramp-up — often well north of a quarter-million a year before they've delivered a thing. And for most companies, the role is only genuinely full-time during bursts: a re-platform, a funding round, a turnaround.

| Full-time CTO hire | Fractional CTO — Digital One Agency |
|--------------------|-------------------------------------|
| $250k+ all-in, every year, regardless of workload | Pay only for the leadership you actually need |
| 3–6 months to recruit, more to ramp | Productive in week one — no ramp, no learning curve |
| One person's experience, often from one industry | 27 years across five industries, in one operator |
| Hard and expensive to unwind if it's the wrong fit | Scale up for a project, scale down when it's done |
| Frequently under-utilised once the big project lands | Founder-grade accountability without the headcount |

Fractional isn't "CTO-lite." It's senior leadership matched to the moments that need it — and an honest advisor whose incentive is your outcome, not protecting a permanent seat.
