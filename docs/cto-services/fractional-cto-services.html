01 — The case

Why a CEO should read this

The real bottleneck is rarely the technology itself — it's who is making the calls.

You don't have a technology problem. You have a decision-making problem with technology at the centre of it — which platform to bet on, what to build versus buy, where the next dollar of margin hides, and whether the team you have can take you where the board wants to go.

Most companies answer that by hiring an expensive full-time CTO, or by leaving the call to whoever shouts loudest in the engineering room. There is a third option that more boards are choosing: bring in someone who has already owned the whole problem — the building, the billing, the infrastructure, the financing and the exit — and rent the judgement, not the headcount.

I've signed the front of the cheques and the back of them. I've built the product, run the network it rode on, and financed the customers who bought it. There aren't many technology decisions I'm seeing for the first time.

04 — The model

Why fractional works

A full-time CTO at the seniority you actually want costs base salary, equity, bonus, super and a long tail of recruitment and ramp-up — often well north of a quarter-million a year before delivering a thing. And for most companies the role is only genuinely full-time in bursts.

Full-time CTO hire

High fixed cost · long commitment
  • $250k+ all-in, every year, regardless of workload
  • 3–6 months to recruit, more to ramp
  • One person's experience, often from one industry
  • Hard and expensive to unwind if it's the wrong fit
  • Frequently under-utilised once the big project lands

Fractional CTO — Digital One

Flexible cost · judgement on tap
  • Pay only for the leadership you actually need
  • Productive in week one — no ramp, no learning curve
  • 27 years across five industries, in one operator
  • Scale up for a project, scale down when it's done
  • Founder-grade accountability without the headcount

Fractional isn't "CTO-lite." It's senior leadership matched to the moments that need it — and an honest advisor whose incentive is your outcome, not protecting a permanent seat.
